# 00 — Brief

## Original user idea (verbatim)

> "I'd like to see a matriarchy thoughtfully done. This goes for worldbuilding
> in general, I suppose. Changing one small thing in reality can have
> dramatic ramification that ripple through society, history, etc. Too often
> it seems like a book is just 14th century England + magic. (Could be
> beneficial for the magic system as women are more in touch with their
> emotions than men - play off that stereotype)
>
> Female leads who are allowed to be goofy. They usually have to be badass
> stone cold killing machines who are impossibly beautiful and everyone falls
> in love with. Give me a total dork please! Maybe a hero who is a huge nerd
> about potion making or goes googoo at the sight of dragons or is just
> totally uncool in every way. Men and women really, but I feel female leads
> get pressured to be badass warriors."

## Inferred direction

A secondary-world fantasy built on the existing `novel-bible` magic system
(The Permeance), in which a centuries-old, mechanically-derived matriarchy —
Aldermere, governed by the Riverbed Council and the Court of Open Truth — is
the backdrop for a small, personal rescue story. The protagonist, Wren
Wickern, is an alchemist (Material-opener) — by design the least prestigious,
least socially entangled kind of practitioner in her world, and explicitly
not on the Court/Council track the matriarchy expects of gifted women. She is
a dork: obsessed with wyrm lore and alchemical chemistry, more comfortable
with a retort and a ledger than a room full of people. When her sister Mari —
who *did* walk the prestige path, and is exactly the luminous, composed,
self-sacrificing healer the culture asks its women to be — overextends
herself past safe recovery and begins sliding toward Dissolution, Wren has to
go looking for a cure that doesn't exist in any guild ledger, and the only
person who has ever solved this exact problem is a woman the Court quietly
considers a cautionary tale.

## Language

English.

## Genre

Secondary-world fantasy with a hard, internally consistent magic system;
tonally warm, funny, and character-forward rather than grimdark. Comp
register: Uprooted, Spinning Silver, T. Kingfisher's adult fantasy, Robin
McKinley.

## Audience

Adult and upper-YA crossover readers who want a fantasy that takes its own
premises seriously — a matriarchy with a real mechanism and a real cost, not
a costume change — and who are tired of female leads who are only allowed to
be competent if they're also a stone-cold warrior. Readers who want to laugh
at the protagonist's expense (affectionately) between gut-punches.

## Target length

~95,000–105,000 words across 20 chapters.

## Narrative mode

Third person limited, single POV (Wren Wickern), past tense, voicey and wry.

## Reader promise

*A world where the rules of magic built the rules of society, brick by
mundane brick — and a heroine who is allowed to love her weird little hobby
more than she loves looking good, right up until loving someone else costs
her the one defense she's always had.*

The promise has two parts that have to both land: (1) the worldbuilding
payoff — every chapter should make the matriarchy feel more *earned*, never
more decorative — and (2) the character payoff — Wren's arc from
people-avoidant dork to someone who opens, fully, on purpose, for someone
else, has to feel like the same kind of bravery the book has spent the whole
runtime insisting it isn't (warrior bravery), which is the entire thesis of
the brief.
