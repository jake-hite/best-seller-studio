# 05 — Outline

## Macro structure

Built directly on the bible's 7-point structure (story-structure.md), mapped
across 20 chapters in four movements:

- **Movement I — Hook & Plot Turn 1 (Ch. 1–4):** Wren's closed-off normal,
  broken by Mari's collapse, ending in the guild's refusal and Wren's
  decision to seek Vesper Reed.
- **Movement II — Rising Action & Pinch 1 (Ch. 5–10):** Wren builds an
  unsanctioned theory, attempts a stopgap, and pays the first real,
  embodied cost of staying open to a person.
- **Movement III — Midpoint & Rising to Pinch 2 (Ch. 11–16):** The
  villain-mirror reveal, the seduction of Vesper's competence and warmth,
  the widening of the matriarchy's cost onto Cae's subplot, and Mari's
  near-Dissolution.
- **Movement IV — Plot Turn 2 & Resolution (Ch. 17–20):** Vesper's sincere,
  dangerous offer; the convergent hinge where Wren refuses to be a resource
  and finds Vesper's real dependency; the climax; the costly, warm landing.

## Central conflict (restated for architecture)

Can Wren get Mari back from Saturation without either of them paying for it
in the currency Vesper paid in — and if the only working method on record
requires Severance, is the method actually true, or has everyone, including
Vesper, simply never tried the alternative because the alternative requires
a kind of courage Aldermere doesn't have a name or a Court seat for?

## Tension map

Tension is tracked on two axes that should never both be slack at once:
**Mari's clock** (how close to Dissolution she is, escalating in discrete,
named stages — Bleed → Saturation → deepening Saturation → near-Dissolution
→ resolved) and **Wren's interiority** (how closed vs. open she is to people,
starting closed, dipping further closed under fear in Movement II, cracking
open at the Midpoint, and completing its arc only in Ch.19). The two axes are
designed to cross at the Midpoint (Ch.11) — the chapter where the external
clock and the internal arc both turn on the same fact: Vesper is what Mari
becomes if nothing changes, and what Wren becomes if she takes the easy way
out of fixing it.

## Chapter outline

### Movement I — Hook & Plot Turn 1

**Ch. 1 — A Perfectly Reasonable Amount of Feeling.**
*Function:* establish Wren's voice, her specialization, her closed-toward-
people/open-toward-materials baseline, and the wyrm-scale Chekhov's gun.
*Ending turn:* a runner arrives from Lowmarket — Mari has collapsed.

**Ch. 2 — The Lowmarket Fever.**
*Function:* establish Mari, the fever crisis, and the guild/Court's official
read of Mari's condition (Bleed, recoverable with rest — which turns out to
be wrong). Establish Hesper and the family dynamic.
*Ending turn:* the ward healer admits, quietly, that Mari hasn't actually
been resting, and hasn't for weeks.

**Ch. 3 — The Name Nobody Says at Council.**
*Function:* Wren goes looking for precedent for a Saturation this advanced;
hits a wall of institutional silence; finds the one name everyone flinches
at.
*Ending turn:* Wren decides to find Vesper Reed, against Hesper's open fear.

**Ch. 4 — What the Guild Won't Sign For.**
*Function:* Wren asks Corvin for the guild's sanction and resources to
pursue this; he refuses, on the record, and means it as protection.
*Ending turn (Plot Turn 1 proper):* Wren leaves the guild-house without his
blessing or his keys, for the first time in her professional life choosing
her sister over her standing.

### Movement II — Rising Action & Pinch 1

**Ch. 5 — Wickern & Daughters.**
*Function:* home-front chapter; Hesper's guilt and history recontextualize
Wren's childhood wound; Wren learns Mari was never simply "naturally"
selfless.
*Ending turn:* Hesper, against her own fear, gives Wren her grandmother's
full wyrm-resonance journal — the thing she always assumed Wren would
"grow out of" wanting.

**Ch. 6 — The Locked Cabinet.**
*Function:* Corvin, having refused officially, helps unofficially —
restores the alliance the chapter 4 break threatened, on his terms.
*Ending turn:* Wren has the guild's confirmed wyrm-scale ledger and Corvin's
unspoken blessing, but not his hands-on help.

**Ch. 7 — A Theory With No Guild Behind It.**
*Function:* Wren builds the actual theory: a wyrm-resonance buffer that
might let a Material-opener safely interpose between a Person-opener and
the thing flooding them, untested on anything but inert material.
*Ending turn:* the theory works, on paper, well enough to be genuinely
dangerous to try.

**Ch. 8 — The House on the Quiet Ward.**
*Function:* short, tense; Wren brings the theory and the scale to Mari's
sickroom against the ward healer's better judgment.
*Ending turn:* Mari, lucid enough to understand what's being attempted,
says yes anyway.

**Ch. 9 — What It Costs to Hold Open a Person. (Pinch 1 — Bleed event)**
*Function:* Wren attempts the buffer directly on Mari for the first time;
it works, partially, and Wren Bleeds doing it — the chapter's back half is
written from inside the Bleed, the book's first fully embodied overextension
sequence.
*Ending turn:* Mari is stabilized, not healed — bought time, not a cure —
and Wren understands, in her own body now, exactly what three years of
Mari's career has cost.

**Ch. 10 — Recovery, As Such.**
*Function:* Wren recovers (per the bible's actual recovery mechanics: rest,
solitude, reciprocal opening, uncharged environment); intercut with reports
that Mari is slipping again despite the buffer.
*Ending turn:* Wren accepts that a stopgap isn't a cure, and commits to
finding Vesper Reed directly rather than working around her.

### Movement III — Midpoint & Rising to Pinch 2

**Ch. 11 — The Magistrate Who Used to Be Someone. (Midpoint)**
*Function:* Wren finds Vesper; the chapter's structure is built so Wren
recognizes the shape of Vesper's old Court file (golden, overworked,
beloved, gone past the edge in open session) a half-beat before Vesper
confirms it. The mirror is the point — Vesper is what Mari is becoming and
what Wren could become by taking the fast way out.
*Ending turn:* Vesper agrees to help — for now, on her own terms, naming no
price yet.

**Ch. 12 — Reciprocity.**
*Function:* the seduction chapter. Vesper is generous, exact, and genuinely
useful; she validates Wren's anger at the matriarchy's prestige ideal in a
way no one in Wren's life ever has.
*Ending turn:* Vesper teaches Wren a real, working refinement to the buffer
technique — and asks for nothing back yet, which is its own kind of hook.

**Ch. 13 — Wyrmsign. (joy grace-note)**
*Function:* Vesper takes Wren to confirm wyrmsign in the old quarries
outside Confluence — a rare, wondrous, dorky-joy sequence that deepens trust
between them and reseeds the wyrm-material resource for the climax.
*Ending turn:* Wren, happier than she's been since this began, lets her
guard down completely with Vesper — the chapter's warmth is the trap.

**Ch. 14 — The Reform Faction.**
*Function:* Councilwoman Iris Hale and Cae Rookwood's subplot escalate to
full weight; Wren learns Vesper has a documented history of pulling young
practitioners toward Severance "for their own good," including some who
regretted it.
*Ending turn:* the first crack in Wren's trust in Vesper — small, deniable,
noted and set aside rather than confronted.

**Ch. 15 — Lowmarket, Again.**
*Function:* short, fast; the buffer technique's limits show — it was always
going to fail eventually, and it does.
*Ending turn:* Mari is worse than she has ever been.

**Ch. 16 — Going Under. (Pinch 2 — near-Dissolution)**
*Function:* Mari nearly goes under for real; the chapter's low point, the
closest the book comes to the tragedy it explicitly chose not to be.
*Ending turn:* Mari is pulled back, barely, by Wren and the ward healer
together — bought, at most, days.

**Ch. 17 — The Offer.**
*Function:* Vesper, having watched Ch.16 unfold, offers the actual cure
outright — and is honest, on page, that it requires Wren to begin her own
Severance, framed sincerely as mercy, not as a trick.
*Ending turn:* Wren says she needs to think, and means it — the offer is
genuinely tempting, not a strawman.

### Movement IV — Plot Turn 2 & Resolution

**Ch. 18 — The Dependency. (Plot Turn 2 — convergent hinge)**
*Function:* Vesper asks Wren to let herself be used as the conduit — framed
as Wren's choice, but structured, on inspection, so that Vesper needs a
genuinely open partner to make the technique work at all. Wren realizes
Vesper's Severance was never self-sufficient; it has always required
borrowing someone else's still-open heart, which is the one thing Vesper
can no longer supply herself.
*Ending turn:* Wren refuses to be Severed and refuses to leave — she offers
to be the open partner Vesper's method needs, on her own terms, fully aware
of the risk, instead of as a resource to be used.

**Ch. 19 — The Open Hand. (Resolution — climax)**
*Function:* mirror-inverse of Ch.1/Ch.9. Wren opens fully toward Mari — and,
in the technique's final turn, toward Vesper too, the first person to do so
since Vesper's own Severance — at full, on-purpose risk, no buffer, no
half-measure. Mari is pulled back for good. The cost is real: Wren is left
permanently more porous to people than she was, unable to fully close the
way she used to, a fair trade she chose with her eyes open.
*Ending turn:* Vesper, exposed to a genuine open feeling for the first time
in decades, is left undefended — not destroyed, not redeemed, just open,
possibly for the first time fully facing what she has done.

**Ch. 20 — What Stays Open. (Denouement)**
*Function:* settle the household, Mari's long recovery and changed
relationship to her own selflessness, Cae's situation given a real if
incomplete resolution via Iris Hale's reform push, Wren and Bram's
low-heat warmth allowed to be exactly as uncool and lovely as the brief
asked for.
*Ending turn:* final image mirrors Ch.1's opening image inverted — Wren
alone in her workshop, but this time leaves the door open on purpose,
mid-page, for no urgent reason at all.
