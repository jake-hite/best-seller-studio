# 01 — Market Map

## Market signals

- Strong, sustained reader demand for "cozy-adjacent" secondary-world
  fantasy: lower body count, higher interiority, humor used as a release
  valve rather than a tonal mask. Readers are explicitly fatigued with
  grimdark-by-default and with magic systems that are decorative rather than
  load-bearing.
- A standing, vocal appetite — visible in romance-adjacent fantasy and in
  reader discourse around "strong female character" fatigue — for female
  leads who are allowed competence *without* combat-coded badassery: nerds,
  bookworms, tradeswomen, healers, scholars who are the hero of their own
  story without ever picking up a sword.
- Matriarchal or gender-inverted societies are a recurring fantasy
  subgenre move, but reader/critic complaints about the subgenre converge on
  one failure mode: the inversion is cosmetic ("patriarchy with a queen"),
  with no mechanism and no cost. A matriarchy with a traceable cause and a
  real, named institutional flaw is a differentiator, not just a hook.

## Comparable titles

1. **Uprooted** (Naomi Novik) — a magic system with real interiority cost,
   an unglamorous protagonist defined by competence rather than beauty, and
   worldbuilding where geography and magic are inseparable. Shares this
   project's instinct that magic should have *texture* and *consequence*,
   not just spectacle.
2. **Spinning Silver** (Naomi Novik) — multi-POV, economically-literate
   fantasy where social structures (debt, trade, marriage) are as load-bearing
   as magic. Shares this project's interest in showing how power
   *actually* accumulates and gets inherited, not just who holds a crown.
3. **Nettle & Bone / the Saint of Steel books** (T. Kingfisher) — adult
   fantasy whose heroines are allowed to be plain, tired, funny, and
   un-chosen-one, and whose humor coexists with real horror and real cost.
   Closest tonal comp for "let the dork be goofy without undercutting the
   stakes."
4. **The Tea Dragon Society / general "cottagecore-adjacent" fantasy
   wave** — signals reader appetite for craft-obsessed protagonists
   (potion-making, tinkering, small creatures) as a legitimate genre
   register rather than comic relief. Validates the alchemist-protagonist
   choice commercially, not just thematically.

## Recurring patterns across comps

- The protagonist's "uncool" trait is never just color — it becomes the
  mechanism that solves the climax (Agnieszka's untrained intuition in
  Uprooted, Miryem's bookkeeping in Spinning Silver). This project's wyrm
  alchemy obsession is built the same way: a hobby that turns out to be the
  only expertise capable of solving the actual plot problem.
- Matriarchal/gender-inverted worldbuilding lands best when the book is also
  honest about what the system costs *the gender it favors*, not just the
  one it disadvantages — readers reward the double edge over a simple
  inversion-as-wish-fulfillment.
- Villains read as strongest in this comp space when they are a *plausible
  future* for the protagonist or a beloved character, not an external evil.

## Whitespace opportunity

No major current title combines (a) a matriarchy with a fully mechanical,
non-cosmetic cause, (b) a protagonist whose "uncool hobby" is structurally
tied to the lowest-prestige path in her own society's gender politics
specifically *because* it's gender-mixed and unglamorous, and (c) a magic
system where the antagonist's power source is a moral choice (severing
obligation from feeling) rather than a quantity of power, making villainy a
question of ethics rather than strength. This is the gap this project sits
in: matriarchy-with-a-mechanism + dork-coded competence + a villain who is a
warning rather than a wall to knock down.
