# 07 — Opening Strategy

## Strategy

Open inside Wren's specialization at full, unguarded competence — not
explaining the magic system, but demonstrating it through a small, specific,
funny act of expertise (handling something dangerous well, for reasons that
matter only to her) — so the reader's first impression of the protagonist is
*good at this, happy, a little ridiculous, not performing for anyone.* This
is the chapter's "first true opening as joy" beat as well as its
characterization engine: showing Wren open fully toward a *thing* before any
character has reason to ask her to open toward a *person* establishes the
hook/resolution mirror (07 ties directly to ASSUMPTIONS.md decision #6)
without a single line of exposition about it.

The chapter must also, without naming the matriarchy as a concept, make its
mechanics visible: a passing reference to Wren's own permeability test, to
why her specialization is the "useful hands" path, to the gendered shape of
who's in the room and who isn't, should all land as scenery, not lecture.

## First line options (selected: Option 2)

1. *"Wren had been told, more than once, that it was unprofessional to name
   her equipment, and more than once she had explained that Bartholomew had
   never once let her down, which was more than could be said for most of
   the people who'd told her that."*
2. *"The trick to handling a wyrm scale that was still faintly, technically
   alive was to want it less than it wanted to be left alone, and Wren had
   spent four years getting genuinely, professionally bad at wanting
   things."*
3. *"Nobody had ever been impressed by Wren Wickern, which suited her fine,
   right up until the morning it didn't."*

**Selected: Option 2.** It does four jobs at once — establishes voice (dry,
precise, self-aware), establishes the magic system's central mechanic
(wanting/feeling as the literal operative force) in concrete sensory terms
rather than as lore, establishes Wren's core wound obliquely ("gotten bad at
wanting things" as a joke that is also exactly true), and earns its own
second sentence by being specific enough to demand explanation rather than
complete enough to not need one.

## First page promise

The first page must deliver: (1) a working demonstration of the magic
system's central mechanic — opening/wanting/feeling as the literal fuel —
without a glossary; (2) Wren's specific, lived-in competence at something
the people around her don't value; (3) a hint that this competence is also
a hiding place, planted lightly enough that it reads as charm on a first
pass and as foreshadowing on a second. By the bottom of page one, a reader
should know what kind of book this is — funny, specific, magic-literate,
character-first — without yet knowing what's about to go wrong.

## Chapter one ending

Chapter one ends with the Lowmarket runner's arrival and the bare fact of
Mari's collapse — not a cliffhanger sentence fragment, but a hard, clean turn
that converts every detail the chapter just spent establishing (Wren's
competence, her closed-off comfort, her relationship to her own gift) into
a question the reader now needs answered: *what happens when the thing
Wren is good at isn't the thing this requires.* Per the architecture rule
that a chapter-one ending must increase forward pressure rather than merely
end the scene, the chapter does not pause to let Wren react — it ends on the
news itself, mid-motion, so the reader's only option is to turn the page.

## Opening quality checks (per architecture rules)

- First line earns the second: confirmed — option 2's specificity ("still
  faintly, technically alive") cannot be fully parsed without continuing.
- Opening establishes voice, tension, and care in the first scene: voice via
  diction/rhythm; tension via the literal danger of the scale-handling task;
  care via Wren's evident attachment to her work and her dry but real
  affection for Corvin, glimpsed in the same scene.
- Chapter one ending increases forward pressure: confirmed — it does not
  resolve or soften, it redirects all established stakes onto a new,
  unanswered question.
